Most research on clientelism treats parties as active buyers and voters as passive targets. We argue that this misses vote selling, where voters initiate transactions and price their support. Building on prospect theory, we develop a formal model in which initiative shifts bargaining power, perceived electoral risk, and surplus allocation. Loss aversion provides one mechanism through which electorally exposed parties may overpay to avoid defeat. A laboratory experiment varying initiative, ideology, electoral strength, and pivotality supports the model’s behavioral implications. Party-initiated vote buying concentrates offers among ideologically proximate voters and generates higher voter payoffs. Voter-initiated vote selling shifts requested prices toward electorally advantaged parties, especially when they are ideologically distant, but exposes voters to rejection risk and does not necessarily increase voter surplus.
